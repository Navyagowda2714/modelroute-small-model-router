## Label taxonomy

| Category | Intents |
|---|---|
| billing | billing_double_charge, refund_request, invoice_request, payment_failed, update_payment_method |
| account | login_issue, password_reset, account_locked, update_account_details, delete_account |
| subscription | cancel_subscription, upgrade_plan, downgrade_plan |
| shipping | shipping_delay, track_order, wrong_item, damaged_item, change_shipping_address |
| technical | app_crash, feature_request |

Intents are sampled uniformly (about 190-225 per intent in train).

**Priority rule.** The priority starts at the intent's base priority (`taxonomy.py`) and is then adjusted:
- A severe cue (chargeback, legal threat, business down; 4% of tickets) sets it to **urgent**.
- An urgency cue ("ASAP", "third time", "team is blocked"; 20%) raises it by one level.
- A calm cue ("no rush"; 14%) lowers it by one level.

Train distribution: low 1,331, medium 1,479, high 887, urgent 312.

## How the text is generated

1. **Body.** A template is chosen for the intent and its slots are filled: amounts, order IDs, products, plans, dates, apps and card descriptions. There are 9-11 templates per intent. About two per intent are deliberately written with vocabulary shared with a confusable intent (cancel vs downgrade, login vs password reset vs locked, delay vs tracking, wrong vs damaged).
2. **Difficulty type:**
   - `multi_intent` (10%): a second request from another intent is appended after "Also," / "Side note -". Half the time it is a full sentence. **The label is the first (primary) request.**
   - `negation` (7%): a decoy clause negates a confusable intent ("I'm not trying to cancel my subscription"), followed by the true request.
   - `long` (6%): 2-4 filler sentences of irrelevant context.
   - `simple`: everything else.
3. **Paraphrase.** Word-level synonym substitution (charged -> billed, order -> purchase, ...).
4. **Framing.** An optional greeting and sign-off, plus the priority cue sentence.
5. **Surface noise.** Typos (15% of tickets: swapped, dropped or doubled characters), lower-casing (12%) and punctuation stripping (8%).
6. **Label noise (deliberate).** Priority is shifted by one level in 3% of tickets and intent is swapped within the same category in 1%. The `label_noise` field records which labels were corrupted, so the evaluation can treat them correctly.
7. **Duplicates.** 2% of tickets are exact re-submissions of the previous one, and cleaning removes them again.

## Split strategy: template-group holdout

A random split of template data lets the same sentence skeletons appear in train and test. The first version of this project measured 99% accuracy that way, which says nothing about generalisation. Instead:

- For each intent, 2 templates are chosen as **holdout**, and 8% of tickets are written from them.
- Holdout tickets go **only** into val/test. Seen-template tickets fill train and the rest of val/test, stratified by intent.
- Result: val has 248 holdout + 611 seen tickets, and test has 236 holdout + 623 seen. A unit test enforces that no holdout template appears in train.

## Cleaning

- Unicode NFKC normalisation, zero-width character removal and whitespace collapsing.
- Tickets shorter than 3 characters are dropped (0 in practice).
- Exact duplicates are removed after case-folding (273 removed).

## Intended use and limitations

- **Intended use:** benchmarking routing, calibration and cost trade-offs, and demonstrating the fine-tuning pipeline end to end.
- **Not intended for:** training a production triage model as-is. Real tickets are longer, multilingual, contain PII and attachments, and their label noise is structured (annotators disagree systematically), not uniform.
- **English only.** Names and products are generic.
- Because generation rules are known, rule-based baselines could be overfit to them. The offline keyword stand-in in `models/large.py` is *not* used for any reported number for that reason.
